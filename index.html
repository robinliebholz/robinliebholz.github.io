About

Hi! I am Robin, a PhD Candidate and Research & Teaching Associate in the Department of Economics at the University of Cologne. My advisors are Felix Bierbrauer and Johannes Münster.

I am an applied theorist whose research is centered on how to improve and optimally design government policies. I mainly — but not exclusively — use methods from optimal tax theory and mechanism design. Besides, I am interested in positive questions on how political actors really behave and on the intersection of economics and philosophy.

Outside economics, I enjoy sports — these days surfing, football, calisthenics, yoga — the arts and philosophy.

Work in Progress

Bureaucracy and Social Transfer Systems

single-authored

Summary

I construct a model of optimal social transfers with costly bureaucracy. Bureaucracy makes individualized (case-by-case) transfers possible, but it is costly — for the state, because of screening applicants, administering individual cases and running the system, and for agents, because of the reporting burden. The state would like to individualize transfers because agents differ in their marginal utility of money, which is higher for agents with greater need. Even when the state can observe need, it is optimal to give at least some range of agents a uniform transfer. When need is unobservable, this range is even larger. In regions with individualized transfers, agents with lower need receive an information rent. Informational constraints can also make it optimal to have more brackets of uniform transfers. This offers an explanation for the multi-bracket structure of many real-world transfer systems.
